Combine two networks into one

The first network outputs \(y\), which is the input to the second network.

\[ \begin{aligned} h_i &= a[\theta_{i0}+\theta_{i1}x], && i=1,2,3\\ y &= \phi_0+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\phi_j h_j\\ h'_i &= a[\theta'_{i0}+\theta'_{i1}y], && i=1,2,3\\ y' &= \phi'_0+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\phi'_j h'_j \end{aligned} \]

Substitute the first output into the second hidden layer:

\[h'_i=a\!\left[\theta'_{i0}+\theta'_{i1}\phi_0+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\theta'_{i1}\phi_jh_j\right].\]

Create new variables

Define

\[\psi_{i0}=\theta'_{i0}+\theta'_{i1}\phi_0, \qquad \psi_{ij}=\theta'_{i1}\phi_j,\quad i,j=1,2,3.\]

Then the hidden units of the second network become

\[ \begin{aligned} h'_1&=a[\psi_{10}+\psi_{11}h_1+\psi_{12}h_2+\psi_{13}h_3]\\ h'_2&=a[\psi_{20}+\psi_{21}h_1+\psi_{22}h_2+\psi_{23}h_3]\\ h'_3&=a[\psi_{30}+\psi_{31}h_1+\psi_{32}h_2+\psi_{33}h_3] \end{aligned} \]

Two-layer network

\[ \begin{aligned} h_1 &= a[\theta_{10}+\theta_{11}x] & h'_1 &= a[\psi_{10}+\psi_{11}h_1+\psi_{12}h_2+\psi_{13}h_3]\\ h_2 &= a[\theta_{20}+\theta_{21}x] & h'_2 &= a[\psi_{20}+\psi_{21}h_1+\psi_{22}h_2+\psi_{23}h_3]\\ h_3 &= a[\theta_{30}+\theta_{31}x] & h'_3 &= a[\psi_{30}+\psi_{31}h_1+\psi_{32}h_2+\psi_{33}h_3] \end{aligned} \]

\[y'=\phi'_0+\phi'_1h'_1+\phi'_2h'_2+\phi'_3h'_3\]

Two-layer network as one equation

\[ \begin{aligned} h_i &= a[\theta_{i0}+\theta_{i1}x], & i&=1,2,3,\\ h'_i &= a\!\left[\psi_{i0}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\psi_{ij}h_j\right], & i&=1,2,3,\\ y' &= \phi'_0+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\phi'_i h'_i. \end{aligned} \]

Combining the layers:

\[ \boxed{\displaystyle y' = \phi'_0+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\phi'_i\, a\!\left[\psi_{i0}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\psi_{ij} a(\theta_{j0}+\theta_{j1}x)\right] } \]

Hyperparameters

  • \(K\) layers = depth of network

  • \(D_k\) hidden units per layer = width of network

  • These are called hyperparameters — chosen before training the network.

  • Can try retraining with different hyperparameters: hyperparameter optimization or hyperparameter search.

Notation change #1 — building matrix notation

\[ \begin{aligned} h_i &= a[\theta_{i0}+\theta_{i1}x],\quad i=1,2,3\\ h'_i &= a[\psi_{i0}+\psi_{i1}h_1+\psi_{i2}h_2+\psi_{i3}h_3],\quad i=1,2,3\\ y' &= \phi'_0+\phi'_1h'_1+\phi'_2h'_2+\phi'_3h'_3 \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{bmatrix}h_1\\h_2\\h_3\end{bmatrix} =a\!\left[\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{10}\\\theta_{20}\\\theta_{30}\end{bmatrix} +\begin{bmatrix}\theta_{11}\\\theta_{21}\\\theta_{31}\end{bmatrix}x\right] \]

\[h'_i=a\!\left[\psi_{i0}+\sum_{j=1}^3\psi_{ij}h_j\right],\quad i=1,2,3\]

\[y'=\phi'_0+\sum_{i=1}^3\phi'_ih'_i\]

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbf h &= a[\boldsymbol\theta_0+\boldsymbol\theta x]\\[3mm] \begin{bmatrix}h'_1\\h'_2\\h'_3\end{bmatrix} &= a\!\left[ \begin{bmatrix}\psi_{10}\\\psi_{20}\\\psi_{30}\end{bmatrix} +\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{11}&\psi_{12}&\psi_{13}\\\psi_{21}&\psi_{22}&\psi_{23}\\\psi_{31}&\psi_{32}&\psi_{33}\end{bmatrix} \mathbf h\right] \end{aligned}\]

\[y'=\phi'_0+\sum_{i=1}^3\phi'_ih'_i\]

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbf h &= a[\boldsymbol\theta_0+\boldsymbol\theta x]\\[3mm] \mathbf h' &= a[\boldsymbol\psi_0+\boldsymbol\Psi\mathbf h]\\[3mm] y' &= \phi'_0+\begin{bmatrix}\phi'_1&\phi'_2&\phi'_3\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}h'_1\\h'_2\\h'_3\end{bmatrix} \end{aligned}\]

Notation change #2

Introduce vectors and matrices:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf h &= a[\boldsymbol\theta_0+\boldsymbol\theta x]\\[2mm] \mathbf h' &= a[\boldsymbol\psi_0+\boldsymbol\Psi\mathbf h]\\[2mm] y' &= \phi'_0+\boldsymbol\phi'\mathbf h' \end{aligned} \]

Notation change #3 — layer indexing

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf h_1 &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_0+\boldsymbol\Omega_0\mathbf x]\\ \mathbf h_2 &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_1+\boldsymbol\Omega_1\mathbf h_1]\\ \mathbf y &= \boldsymbol\beta_2+\boldsymbol\Omega_2\mathbf h_2 \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf h_1 &= a[\underbrace{\boldsymbol\beta_0}_{\text{bias vector}}+ \underbrace{\boldsymbol\Omega_0}_{\text{weight matrix}}\mathbf x]\\ \mathbf h_2 &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_1+\boldsymbol\Omega_1\mathbf h_1]\\ \mathbf y &= \boldsymbol\beta_2+\boldsymbol\Omega_2\mathbf h_2 \end{aligned} \]

General equations for deep network

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf h_1 &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_0+\boldsymbol\Omega_0\mathbf x]\\ \mathbf h_2 &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_1+\boldsymbol\Omega_1\mathbf h_1]\\ \mathbf h_3 &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_2+\boldsymbol\Omega_2\mathbf h_2]\\ &\,\vdots\\ \mathbf h_K &= a[\boldsymbol\beta_{K-1}+\boldsymbol\Omega_{K-1}\mathbf h_{K-1}]\\ \mathbf y &= \boldsymbol\beta_K+\boldsymbol\Omega_K\mathbf h_K \end{aligned} \]

Equivalently, the network computes a composition of affine transformations and activations.

Shallow vs. deep networks

The best results are created by deep networks with many layers.

  • 50–1000 layers for most applications
  • Best results in:
    • Computer vision
    • Natural language processing
    • Graph neural networks
    • Generative models
    • Reinforcement learning

All use deep networks. But why?

Shallow vs. deep networks

1. Ability to approximate different functions?

Both obey the universal approximation theorem.

Argument: One layer is enough, and for deep networks we could arrange for the other layers to compute the identity function.

Shallow vs. deep networks

2. Number of linear regions per parameter

  • Deep networks create many more regions per parameter.
  • But there are dependencies between them:
    • Think of folding example.
    • Perhaps similar symmetries in real-world functions? Unknown.

Shallow vs. Deep Networks

3. Depth efficiency

  • There are some functions that require a shallow network with exponentially more hidden units than a deep network to achieve an equivalent approximation.
  • This is known as the depth efficiency of deep networks.
  • But do the real-world functions we want to approximate have this property? Unknown.

Shallow vs. Deep Networks

4. Large structured networks

  • Think about images as input — might be 1M pixels.
  • Fully connected networks are not practical.
  • Answer is to have weights that only operate locally, and share across image.
  • This leads to convolutional networks.
  • Gradually integrate information from across the image — needs multiple layers.

Shallow vs. Deep Networks

5. Fitting and generalization

  • Fitting of deep models seems to be easier up to about 20 layers.
  • Then needs various tricks to train deeper networks, so (in vanilla form), fitting becomes harder.
  • Generalization is good in deep networks. Why?

Fitting of deep models is also faster.
